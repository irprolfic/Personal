Case study 01 / ProofFeed

A quick check. A clearer picture.

Origin Check explores whether showing the earliest known appearance of footage can help people assess a short news video’s context before believing or sharing its claim.

Focus Short-form news verification
Target user Corroboration Checkers
Stage Discovery and proposed validation
01 / Problem

Real footage can still tell the wrong story.

A news-related short video can leave viewers unsure about when it was recorded, where it happened, or whether its caption matches the event shown. Resolving those questions often means leaving the video and searching elsewhere.

Working problem framing: viewers who want corroboration lack a quick way to connect the footage they are watching with evidence about its original context.

The scope is consumer verification of short-form news, particularly while watching or before sharing.

02 / Research

People check, but their stopping points differ.

Earlier interview discussions surfaced these qualitative signals. They do not establish how common each behavior is across the wider population.

Search is a common workaround.

Participants described using Google or YouTube to investigate claims encountered in short videos.

Verification has a time limit.

Some described checking within about a minute; another account involved 10–15 minutes of searching.

A familiar publisher can become a stopping point.

Matching coverage from established news outlets served as a trust signal in some interview accounts.

Uncertainty does not always lead to checking.

Accounts also included ignoring a video or changing a purchase decision without verification.

Draft evidence summary: survey counts and final interview coding are not reproduced here.

03 / Product decision

Focus on context that can inform a decision.

The proposed audience is Corroboration Checkers: viewers who already want supporting evidence. Origin Check focuses on footage history and caption mismatches.

The hypothesis is that an accessible origin record will help these viewers decide whether the footage supports the claim. That connection still needs to be tested.

04 / Proposed experience

Bring the context beside the video.

An Origin Check action sits beside controls such as Like and Share. It opens a compact record with the earliest known appearance of the footage, available date and location evidence, and potential caption mismatches.

Information Question it helps answer
Earliest known appearance Has this footage appeared before?
Date and location evidence Does the available context fit the current claim?
Caption mismatch Is the footage being presented with conflicting context?
Traceable source What evidence can I inspect myself?

“Earliest known” describes what the system has found. It does not establish the first-ever upload or prove every claim made in the video.

05 / Validation plan

Does the record change the decision?

Compare viewers shown an origin record with viewers assessing the same material without one. Observe where they stop checking, what conclusion they reach, and which evidence influenced it.

  • Measure time to a decision and accuracy against established context.
  • Record the evidence used and any additional searches.
  • Track useful results and inconclusive results.
  • Set quantitative decision thresholds before testing.

Treat the P0 as refuted if the record does not change where users stop or how they reach a conclusion, if they find it interesting but irrelevant to belief, or if too many important videos return no useful result.

Open questions include footage coverage, source quality, conflicting evidence, and the risk of misplaced confidence. These are proposed tests, not achieved outcomes.

06 / Learning

Useful evidence must affect the user’s task.

An origin record can be informative without helping someone assess a claim. The next step is to test whether it addresses the uncertainty that actually makes people search.

Case study 02 / YouTube Shorts

Keep discovery connected to the original task.

Exploring recommendation drift for viewers who open short-form video with something specific to learn.

Target user Task-Oriented Seekers
Research Five discovery interviews
Stage Concept draft; validation pending
01 / Problem

A relevant start does not guarantee a useful session.

A viewer may arrive with a specific question, find a relevant short, and then encounter recommendations that drift away from that task. Continuing to scroll becomes less useful, prompting another search or a switch away.

P0 from the discovery work: Task-Oriented Seekers switch away because recommendations drift from what they came to find.

02 / Research

Separate purposeful searching from browsing.

Five discovery interviews surfaced several themes. The key distinction was the viewer’s intent: entertainment and task completion do not necessarily require the same experience.

Recommendation drift

The viewing sequence could move away from the topic that initially brought the user in.

Retrieval workarounds

Research surfaced ways users tried to recover useful information rather than relying on the feed alone.

Feedback and device friction

Inconsistent experiences with “Not Interested” and device switching also appeared in the research.

These are qualitative themes. Theme-level counts are omitted because the final coding is not included in this draft.

03 / Prioritization

Make progress on the task the focus.

The selected problem centers on Task-Oriented Seekers. A useful intervention should help them find relevant information and continue with purpose.

Watch time alone would not establish success. A longer session could reflect useful exploration or difficulty finding an answer.

04 / Concept direction

Make session intent explicit.

Session Intent Anchor explores keeping the viewer’s current task visible and connected to subsequent discovery. This high-level description is a draft; the detailed interaction still needs to be aligned with the final presentation.

Related exploration: Search-to-Shorts

Earlier work explored AI-curated 30–45 second previews with timestamp links to the source video. That exploration is related context, rather than evidence that Session Intent Anchor has been validated.

Open earlier prototype ↗
05 / Proposed validation

Test task progress and recommendation relevance.

Give participants a concrete information-seeking task. Compare their experience with and without an explicit session intent, using matched content conditions.

Measure What it reveals
Task completion Whether viewers found the information they needed.
Perceived relevance Whether subsequent content stayed connected to the task.
Search restarts and exits Whether viewers still needed to restart or switch away.
Effort to express intent Whether the added interaction was worth its cost.

If users still lose relevance or find the added step burdensome, the concept needs to be reconsidered. No experiment results are claimed here.
